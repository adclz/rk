---
title: Part of a wider address
---
An address inside a wider one the program also names is part of it. Each size counts in its own units and the image is little-endian, so beside a `%QW0`, `%QX0.3` is its bit 3 and `%QB1` its high byte. A part is stored in the wider address's cell and has no storage of its own.

Reading a part, assigning it, and binding an output to it all work, and a declared part may have any type of its width. A part of a byte or more is whole bytes of the cell, so it can also be passed to a `VAR_IN_OUT`, referenced, or count a `FOR` loop. The uses that need storage of its own do not work:

- passing a bit to a `VAR_IN_OUT`, or taking `REF()` of it, which both need an address a bit does not have;
- declaring it `RETAIN` or giving it an initial value, which both belong on the variable located at the wider address.

```iecst
FUNCTION_BLOCK Flip
VAR_IN_OUT v : BOOL; END_VAR
    v := NOT v;
END_FUNCTION_BLOCK

PROGRAM P
VAR f : Flip; w : WORD; END_VAR
    w := %QW0;
    f(v := %QX0.3);
END_PROGRAM
```
